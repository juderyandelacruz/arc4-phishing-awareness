Arc4 Phishing Awareness

Heads up, this was a phishing awareness test.

Good news is, nothing was compromised, no data was collected about you personally, and you're not in any trouble. This was a controlled simulation to help our team get better at spotting phishing emails!

The email you just clicked had these red flags:
1
The sender address was a Gmail address
It came from arc4.security.notice@gmail.com, not @arc4.com. Real internal IT communications come from internal addresses. The display name "Arc4 IT Security" was designed to look legitimate and make you trust it!
2
Generic greeting
"Hi," instead of your name. Internal IT will usually address you specifically, especially for something personal like a password reset!
3
Urgency pressure
"Password expires in 24 hours" is designed to make you act before you think. Real password resets give a reasonable notice!
4
The link destination
If you hovered over the button before clicking, you would notice the link didn't go anywhere connected to Arc4. Always hover before clicking, especially on anything asking for you to log in!
What to do next time you see an email like this

Three habits can catch anything almost instantly!

  • Don't click: Hover over links first to see where they actually go!
  • Report it: You can use Gmail's built-in 'report phishing' option on the email!
  • When in doubt: Verify with the supposed sender through a separate communication channel, call them, slack them, or send them a message!
